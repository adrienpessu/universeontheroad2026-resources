
# How to run the session

## Ship & Tell — nominations, stage showcase and winners

This guide explains how to run Ship & Tell at any Universe on the Road event, from collecting attendee projects at the Terminal station through the main-stage presentations, audience voting and prize announcements.

Use this alongside the [Ship & Tell execution README](README.md). The recommended format is five projects, two rounds of audience voting and prizes for the top three. Applause voting is a simple option that does not require a polling tool.

## The process at a glance

1. Build — Encourage attendees to create something with the GitHub Copilot app.
2. Capture — Save a screenshot or short screen recording with each participant's name and project title.
3. Shortlist — Choose five projects and, where possible, one reserve.
4. Prepare — Transfer project media to the stage laptop and add it to the host deck.
5. Present — Bring the creators on stage for short project pitches.
6. Vote — Involve the audience in selecting the three finalists, then first, second and third place.
7. Celebrate — Award prizes and thank everyone who participated.

## 1. Set up the collection process

Before the Terminal station opens, agree who will receive submissions and where the files will be saved. Use an approved shared folder or designated station laptop. 

For each entry, collect:

- Participant name and project title.
- A screenshot (PNG/JPG) or short screen recording (MP4) showing what they built.

Ask the Terminal team to actively encourage submissions. Small, creative projects are welcome; this is not a competition for the most production-ready software.

## 2. Collect and shortlist the projects

Close submissions at least 30 minutes before the main-stage session. For example, if Ship & Tell begins at 15:35, use a 15:00 cutoff.

At the cutoff:

1. Collect entries from all station laptops into the agreed folder or an authorised USB drive.
2. Review the submissions as a small team and choose five projects based on creativity, usefulness, clear presentation and how GitHub Copilot helped.
3. Select a sixth project as a reserve where possible.
4. Confirm the finalists are still at the venue and happy to present.
5. Record the agreed project order and verify participant names and titles.
6. Hand the ordered list and media files to the host and AV team.

Keep the reserve ready until all five finalists are confirmed on stage. Never discover at the start of the session that a participant has already left.




## 4. Brief the finalists

Bring participants to the stage waiting area before the session and explain the format.

Give each person up to 90 seconds to answer:

- What did you build?
- How did GitHub Copilot help?
- Why is the project useful, interesting or fun?

Explain that their screenshot or recording will be displayed while they speak. No live application setup is required. Ask them to stay nearby for the voting and prize announcements.

A stage assistant should line up the finalists in presentation order and help manage the microphone handoff.

##  Involve the audience in choosing winners

Choose the voting method before the event. Digital polling and applause both work with the same two-round structure.

### Option A — Audience applause

Applause voting is straightforward, interactive and does not require attendees to scan a QR code.

Round 1 — Choose the three finalists:

1. Display the five project names in a fixed order.
2. Ask the audience to applaud for each project one at a time, using the same wording and listening time.
3. Have two event-team members listen from different parts of the room and help identify the three strongest responses.
4. If the third-place cutoff is unclear, repeat the applause vote between the tied projects.
5. Announce the Final Three.

Round 2 — Decide the podium:

1. Start a fresh round with just the three finalists; Round 1 responses do not carry over.
2. Invite applause for each finalist again.
3. With the two designated listeners, determine first, second and third place.
4. Use a head-to-head applause tie-break if needed.
5. Announce the winners in third-, second-, then first-place order.



### Option B — Digital voting using SLido

Use an approved polling platform if the local team prefers a phone-based vote (i.e. Slido)

- Set up the first vote for all five projects and the second vote for the final three.
- Test the QR code, participant capacity and internet connection.
- Confirm who opens and closes each round and who reads the results.
- Keep results hidden until the host announces them.
- Prepare applause voting as a fallback if the platform is unavailable.

## Present prizes and close

Announce third place, then second place, then the winner. Present the prizes and thank all five finalists, including the two who did not place.

If photography is part of the event, invite the winners to a group photo. Finish by thanking the station and event teams, then direct the audience to the reception or next scheduled activity.

### Suggested host closing

> Let's hear it for everyone who built something today. It's amazing what you can create in an afternoon, and that's what Ship & Tell is all about. Congratulations to our winners, and thank you to everyone who took part!



## During the session

- [ ] Introduce the five creators and pitch format.
- [ ] Display each project while its creator speaks.
- [ ] Run the first vote and announce the Final Three.
- [ ] Run the second vote and determine the podium.
- [ ] Announce third, second and first place.
- [ ] Present prizes, thank everyone and close on time.

## What makes the session work

- The station team actively identifies and encourages participants.
- Simple screenshots and recordings are enough to showcase projects.
- One reserve project protects against last-minute withdrawals.
- The host deck contains the media so the event does not depend on live demos.
- Short pitches keep the focus on what people made.
- Audience voting turns the finale into a shared celebration.

Keep the process simple.
