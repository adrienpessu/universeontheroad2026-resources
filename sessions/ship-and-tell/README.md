# Ship & Tell
## City Execution Summary

**Purpose:** Celebrate what attendees have created using the GitHub Copilot app during the hands-on experience, and finish the event with a fun, audience-driven competition.

**Format:** Five projects compete, the audience selects three finalists, then votes for first, second and third place.

### 1. Before Ship & Tell

The host introduces **Ship & Tell** earlier in the event using the slide provided in the host deck, encouraging attendees to participate.

At the hands-on stations, attendees use the **GitHub Copilot app** to create something useful, interesting or creative. This could be a game, an app that solves a real-world problem, or something that helps overcome a technical challenge.

Station teams should encourage attendees to submit their projects for consideration in Ship & Tell.

For each submission, collect:

- Project name and a short description.
- Attendee name and contact information.
- A screenshot or short screen recording of what they built.
- A brief explanation of how they used the GitHub Copilot app.
- Permission to showcase their work on stage.

**Selecting the Final Five**

Station teams can nominate multiple projects. The local team will then select **five projects** for the final showcase.

Selection should consider creativity, usefulness, how GitHub Copilot was used, and how well the project can be presented to an audience.

Prepare a screenshot or short screen recording for each selected project. Live demos are also welcome, but screenshots or recordings are recommended to minimise technical issues when collecting projects from multiple hands-on stations.

### 2. Live Show

**Introduce the Final Five**

The host introduces Ship & Tell during the closing session, explains the competition and encourages the audience to participate.

**Submission cutoff:** Close submissions at least 30 minutes before the session begins (for example, 3:00 PM for a 3:30 PM session). This allows time to select finalists and prepare their content.

**Showcase the Projects**

Present each project using a screenshot, short recording or live demo.

We encourage cities to **invite the five finalists on stage** to present what they've created.

Each finalist has **90 seconds** to explain what they built, how they used the GitHub Copilot app and why their project is interesting.

If a finalist is unavailable or prefers not to present, the host or station team can showcase the project on their behalf.

Keep presentations short, engaging and consistent across all five projects.

**Round 1: Choose the Final Three**

Invite the audience to vote for their three favourite projects from the five showcased.

Reveal the three finalists, building excitement as each is announced.

**Round 2: Choose the Winners**

Give the audience a quick recap of the final three projects.

Run a new vote to determine first, second and third place. Previous voting results do not carry over into this round.

**The Podium Reveal**

Announce the winners in reverse order:

- 🥉 Third place
- 🥈 Second place
- 🥇 First place

Celebrate the winners, present any available prizes and invite them for a group photograph.

### 3. Audience Voting Options

Each city can choose a voting method that best suits its venue, audience size and available technology.

The goal is to make voting **fun, interactive and inclusive**.

**Option A: Digital Voting — Recommended**

Use [Slido](https://www.slido.com/) or a similar audience polling tool.

- Display a QR code so attendees can vote from their phones.
- In Round 1, ask attendees to rank their top three projects from the Final Five.
- Advance the three highest-ranked projects.
- In Round 2, ask attendees to rank the three finalists to determine the podium.
- Keep results hidden until the host is ready to reveal them.

For ranking polls, use a simple points system:

- First preference = 3 points
- Second preference = 2 points
- Third preference = 1 point

The highest total score wins. Reset scores between rounds.

Test the polling tool and confirm its audience capacity before the event.

**Option B: Applause Voting — Fun Alternative**

If digital polling isn't available, turn the voting into a live audience experience.

- Display each project on screen, one at a time.
- Ask the audience to cheer and clap for their favourites.
- Have the host and two supporting staff members assess audience reactions.
- Advance the three projects receiving the strongest reactions.
- For the final round, bring back the three finalists for one last **clap-off**.
- Announce the podium based on audience reaction.

Make it entertaining with a countdown, an applause meter if available, or some friendly encouragement from the host.

Applause voting is intended to be fun and informal. For a more reliable result, consider using counted votes or physical voting tokens instead.

**Option C: Creative Local Voting**

Cities are welcome to introduce their own engaging voting approach, such as:

- Coloured cards or signs held up for each project.
- Physical voting tokens collected in labelled containers.
- Audience members moving to designated voting zones.
- A live cheer-off between finalists.

Whichever method is used, ensure attendees understand the rules, every project receives equal consideration, and a clear approach is agreed for resolving ties.

### 4. Local Team Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Station Teams | Support attendees, collect submissions and nominate projects for the Final Five. |
| Submissions Lead | Review nominations, select the Final Five and coordinate screenshots or recordings. |
| Stage Host | Organise the showcase slides, introduce projects, engage the audience, run voting and announce winners. |
| Marketing / Session Support | Coordinate finalists, prizes, photographs and any additional stage support. |

Roles can be combined depending on the size of the event and available staff.

### 5. What Each City Needs

- Hands-on stations using the GitHub Copilot app.
- A simple way to collect project submissions.
- Five selected projects with screenshots, recordings or prepared demos.
- Slides or another way to showcase the Final Five.
- An agreed audience voting method and tie-break approach.
- Optional prizes or recognition for the three winners.

### 6. After the Event

Encourage cities to share feedback about what worked well, the voting method used and any improvements for future events.

Where attendees have provided permission, share screenshots, project descriptions and photographs of the winning projects with the wider Universe on the Road team.

**The goal is simple: celebrate what attendees shipped, put the audience in control, and end Universe on the Road with something memorable.**
